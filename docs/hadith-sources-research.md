The short terms that matter: Dorar calls its API a way for site owners to “عرض نتائج البحث ... في مواقعهم” ([official page](https://dorar.net/article/389/خدمة-واجهة-الموسوعة-الحديثية-API)); this does not say AI use is permitted. Sunnah.com says “We do not permit the scraping of our data” ([About](https://sunnah.com/about)). Open-Hadith-Data says its database is under the “Open Database License” and its contents under the “Database Contents License” ([license](https://github.com/mhashim6/Open-Hadith-Data/blob/master/LICENSE)). The Jaguar16 dataset states “This dataset is extracted automatically from sunnah.com” ([data license](https://github.com/Jaguar16/open-hadith-data/blob/main/DATA_LICENSE.md)). These statements are why the rights decisions above remain conservative.

## Recommendation

Keep the present approved-source boundary. Ask Dorar whether AI-assisted answer selection, limited quotation, caching and direct result links are allowed, and whether an accessible API path can be provided. In parallel, review the ODbL/DbCL and upstream edition provenance of Open-Hadith-Data. If those are acceptable, compare 50 randomly selected Bukhari/Muslim Arabic text and printed numbers to individually opened Sunnah.com references **before** any index is built. A mismatch must be resolved or excluded. Use permitted translations separately. No new source should be switched on from this research alone.

The failed Dorar request and the uncompleted 20/50 sample targets are evidence gaps, not positive quality results.
