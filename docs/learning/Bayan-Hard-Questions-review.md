## 1. Why am I here?

[Full article in all three languages](Bayan-Hard-Questions-master.md#purpose)

**Original answer:** The Quran gives a clear answer: jinn and human beings were created to worship Allah. Life is also a test that reveals the quality of people’s deeds, so its purpose joins worship of Allah with righteous action.

**Finding:** The original two sentences named worship and testing but did not explain ordinary responsibilities or answer why a self-sufficient God commands worship.

**Change:** Added the immediate provision context, self-sufficiency, moral responsibility, a specific family-care example and three substantive follow-ups.

**Source review:** Core: creation/worship and no provision needed → Q51:56–58; test → Q67:2; dependency → Q35:15. Deeper: worship/kindness → Q4:36; conditional family-spending reward → B56; mindfulness → Q2:21; no increase/decrease of dominion → M2577a. Daily reflection and support are clearly Bayan advice. The sources establish the stated relationship, not an exhaustive account of divine wisdom.

**Language comparison:** Compared dependence direction, conditional intention example, non-exhaustive wisdom claim and all three follow-ups; English completed before Arabic/German equivalents.

**Unresolved:** No claim to explain every divine wisdom or assess personal distress. Independent scholarly/native-language acceptance remains open.

## 2. Who made God?

[Full article in all three languages](Bayan-Hard-Questions-master.md#who-created)

**Original answer:** The Quran describes Allah as independent of creation and denies that He has a parent or offspring. The question treats the Creator as though He had the same description as created things, while the Quran challenges explanations that omit a creator or make people their own creators.

**Finding:** The old answer described independence but did not correct the everything-is-created premise or distinguish explaining a belief from proving it.

**Change:** Added First/nothing-before-Him evidence, explained the premise and dependent/ultimate distinction, and addressed self-creation, special pleading and an uncreated-universe alternative without claiming a complete cosmological proof.

**Source review:** No beginning/First → Q57:3 with M2713a explicit explanation. No parentage/equal → Q112. Dependency → Q35:15. Origin challenge → Q52:35–36. The dependent-maker distinction is labelled Bayan explanatory reasoning; no scientific origin date, exhaustive regress proof or revelation-authentication conclusion is attributed to those passages.

**Language comparison:** Compared the no-self-creation claim, corrected premise, reason/definition distinction, three objections and explicit limits in EN/AR/DE.

**Unresolved:** A full philosophical defence of causal/dependency premises and competing universe models is not claimed. The article answers the alleged internal contradiction and explains the Islamic claim; independent philosophical/source/native review remains open.

## 3. Why is there suffering?

[Full article in all three languages](Bayan-Hard-Questions-master.md#suffering)

**Original answer:** The Quran presents some suffering as a test that can include fear, hunger, and losses affecting wealth, life, and crops. An authentic hadith teaches that a Muslim's fatigue, illness, sorrow, harm, and distress can result in some sins being removed.

**Finding:** Two sentences on testing and expiation did not address the moral objection, children, grief or the difference between explaining suffering and excusing harm.

**Change:** Added final justice, explicit limits on tragedy-specific explanations, childhood accountability, prevention of oppression, prophetic grief and three useful follow-ups.

**Source review:** Testing/loss → Q21:35/Q2:155–157; no divine injustice → Q4:40; final just scales → Q21:47. Preventing a brother’s oppression → B2444, preserving report setting. Child until puberty → AD4403, sahih grade by al-Albani checked. Muslim expiation → B5641–5642, audience preserved. Ibrahim grief and pleasing speech → B1303. Moral/natural distinction and care advice are Bayan explanation, not an asserted divine reason for a case.

**Language comparison:** Compared final-justice scope, child-accountability distinction, Muslim audience of expiation, grief/speech qualifications and no case-specific divine diagnosis across three versions.

**Unresolved:** No exhaustive theodicy or hidden reason for a particular disaster, illness or child’s suffering claimed. Independent scholarly and native-language review remains open.

## 4. Doubt and faith

[Full article in all three languages](Bayan-Hard-Questions-master.md#doubt)

**Original answer:** If your doubts are intrusive thoughts that you dislike and do not accept, their occurrence alone does not show that you are a bad Muslim, the hadith says that Companions had thoughts they found overwhelming and calls that clear faith. Settled doubt about the religion is not the situation described by that hadith, and the Quran describes sincere believers as those who leave doubt behind.

**Finding:** The short answer risked leaving an honest questioner with a contrast between intrusive thoughts and settled doubt without meaningful help or enough interpretive attribution.

**Change:** Explained distinct experiences, attributed the companions’ aversion interpretation, preserved certainty’s place, added Ibrahim’s reassurance setting and practical follow-up on inadequate answers.

**Source review:** Companions’ actual reassurance → M132a; aversion interpretation → Ibn Baz5515, not all doubt is faith. Reflection → Q4:82. Ibrahim’s affirmed belief and reassurance → Q2:260, no equivalence of every doubt. Specific intrusive chain guidance → B3276. Certainty and surrounding claims → Q49:14–16, no individual verdict. Question formulation/help-seeking are Bayan advice.

**Language comparison:** Compared all distinctions, Ibrahim’s affirmation, scope of reassurance and certainty, and absence of a personal faith diagnosis in EN/AR/DE.

**Unresolved:** No classification of an individual’s belief or clinical diagnosis. Philosophical objections still require answers to their actual claims. Independent scholarly/native review remains open.

## 5. Faith and reason

[Full article in all three languages](Bayan-Hard-Questions-master.md#faith-reason)

**Original answer:** The Quran does not ask for blind belief, it points to signs in creation and calls people with understanding to consider them. It also asks rational questions about human origins: were people created without an agent, or did they create themselves?

**Finding:** The original rejected blind belief but mostly listed signs, without teaching how evidence, inference, trust and revelation differ.

**Change:** Added a practical three-claim example, source-trust distinctions, the actual ancestral-following setting and a clear distinction between examining revelation’s authority and learning its content.

**Source review:** Signs/reflection → Q2:164/Q3:190–191/Q4:82; origin challenge → Q52:35–36. No following without knowledge → Q17:36. Criticised ancestral response with lack-of-understanding qualification → Q2:170. Prophetic explanation → Q16:44. Claim/evidence/inference exercise and limits of circular authority are labelled explanatory reasoning, not a religious-status test or empirical finding.

**Language comparison:** Compared religious/historical/logical evidence distinctions, ancestor qualification, revelation’s explanatory role and the same three follow-ups across EN/AR/DE.

**Unresolved:** Does not offer a complete proof of Islam or a comprehensive philosophical epistemology. Each substantive objection still needs its own relevant evidence. Independent source/native review remains open.

## 6. Evolution and Adam

[Full article in all three languages](Bayan-Hard-Questions-master.md#evolution)

**Original answer:** These verses describe Adam as created from dust and clay by God's command. They describe his descendants as coming afterward from an extract of fluid and being formed by God. Ibn Baz rejects the claim that human beings originated as apes and holds that Adam was created from clay.

**Finding:** The old answer gave religious passages and a rejection but did not define evolution or represent empirical human-ancestry research.

**Change:** Added an authoritative definition and a directly inspected primary genome paper, distinguished shared ancestry from a modern-chimp caricature, and preserved the named scholar’s rejection without inventing agreement or a scientific refutation.

**Source review:** Genomic-change definition → NHGRI. Genome comparison/shared ancestry framework → original Nature2005 paper, read from NHGRI-hosted full text because publisher route failed; qualitative summary only, no similarity percentage or age estimate. Adam/dust and descendants → Q3:59/Q32:7–9. Ibn Baz rejection/distinct humanity → actual fatwa20168, not a laboratory finding; his consensus assertion is not independently upgraded into Bayan’s verified consensus. Scope distinctions are explanatory reasoning, not a religious ruling.

**Language comparison:** Compared all three meanings, scientific-source/faith-source separation, actual rejection, common ancestry and no invented reconciliation across EN/AR/DE.

**Unresolved:** No comprehensive scientific literature review or theological reconciliation claimed. The selected scholarly position is attributed, and its relation to evidence is not declared resolved. Specialist scientific/theological and native review remains open.

## 7. Was the Quran preserved?

[Full article in all three languages](Bayan-Hard-Questions-master.md#quran-preserved)

**Original answer:** The Quran says that God Himself sent down the Reminder and will guard it, and it describes the Quran as a revelation that falsehood cannot reach from any angle. Other verses say that its safe collection, recitation, and clarification are with God, so these texts provide the Quran's faith-based foundation for its preservation.

**Finding:** The original used the preservation teaching without historical evidence or an account of readings.

**Change:** Added collection reports including burning, material dating limits, primary manuscript research, textual variation and attributed reading explanation.

**Source review:** [q159](https://quran.com/15/9) supports the religious promise only. [b4986](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4986) and [b4987](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4987) support the stated traditional collection sequence, including dispute and burning. [birm-faq](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/facilities/cadbury/birmingham-quran-mingana-collection/birmingham-quran/faqs) supports two leaves and surah coverage; [birm-research](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/facilities/cadbury/birmingham-quran-mingana-collection/birmingham-quran/research-and-conservation) supports the parchment interval, probability and lack of ink dating. [vanputten2019](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0041977X19000338) supports shared orthography, common written ancestor argument and its explicit distinction of Sanaa lower text; these are the researcher’s historical conclusions. [ib11398](https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/11398/نبذة-مختصرة-عن-علم-القراءات) supports acknowledged letter/vowel/meaning differences and his attributed relationship to the collection. Bayan’s question checklist is editorial guidance, not additional religious or historical proof.

**Language comparison:** Compared all three versions for identical dates, probability, two-leaf scope, text/ink distinction, historical versus religious evidence, variation and attributed interpretation. No invented Quran translation.

**Unresolved:** A full qiraat/ahruf study, direct transcription of individual Sanaa variants, and independent specialist and native-language review remain. This introductory article does not establish every link in oral transmission or settle every textual objection.

## 8. When were hadith written?

[Full article in all three languages](Bayan-Hard-Questions-master.md#hadith-late)

**Original answer:** Ibn Baz holds that writing hadith is permissible and says that permission for it existed during the Prophet's lifetime. He explains that the early restriction guarded against mixing reports with the revealed text and ended after that text became firmly known.

**Finding:** The original only reconciled permission and prohibition, leaving the chronological and reliability concern unanswered.

**Change:** Added specific early-writing reports, an intermediate collection order, later compiler dates, a labelled scholarly reconciliation and the actual scope of authentication.

**Source review:** [b112](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:112) establishes the Abu Shah writing instruction in the conquest sermon setting. [b113](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:113) establishes Abu Hurayrah’s statement about Abdullah’s writing. [ib2419](https://binbaz.org.sa/audios/2419/04-من-حديث-سىل-النبي-صلى-الله-عليه-وسلم-عن-اشياء-كرهها-فلما-اكثر-عليه-غضب) transcribes Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz’s chapter report and gives Ibn Baz’s reconciliation of prohibition/permission. [m3004](https://sunnah.com/muslim:3004) supplies the prohibition itself without an inferred date. [b-about](https://sunnah.com/bukhari/about) supplies compiler dates only, not independent proof of every biographical anecdote on that page. [ib1884](https://binbaz.org.sa/audios/1884/02-من-قوله-فان-خف-الضبط-فالحسن-لذاته) supplies connected transmission, integrity, precision, non-anomaly and freedom from hidden defects. The difference between first recording and compilation is editorial reasoning from these stated stages.

**Language comparison:** All three distinguish the chapter report from a prophetic saying, permission from universal authenticity, reported writing from surviving originals, and scholar reconciliation from hadith wording. Dates and technical-term explanations match.

**Unresolved:** This is a selected chronology, not a comprehensive account of early collections or a physical manuscript chain for every hadith. An independent historian could deepen the intermediate chronology; a disputed individual report still needs its own specialist review. Native-language and scholarly acceptance remain pending.

## 10. Inheritance: do women always receive half?

[Full article in all three languages](Bayan-Hard-Questions-master.md#inheritance)

**Original answer:** The Quran gives a son twice the share of a daughter in some inheritance arrangements, but presents that distribution as a divine obligation and does not state one detailed reason for every case in the inheritance verse. In the family context, the Quran says that a husband spends from his wealth in caring for his wife, that is the financial context stated by the text, not an extra rationale that we should attribute to the verse without a direct statement.

**Finding:** The original acknowledged the limited son/daughter arrangement but offered little explanation and no worked comparison.

**Change:** Added an attributed rationale and two fully assumed examples, showing both a 2:1 case and equal parent shares; distinguished religious prescription from observed household income.

**Source review:** [q47](https://quran.com/4/7) supports prescribed shares for both sexes. [q411](https://quran.com/4/11) supports the son/daughter ratio, parent sixths when a child exists, and prescription; [q41112](https://quran.com/4/11-12) preserves bequests/debt context. [b6732](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6732) supports the remainder rule in example B, where the son is the nearest eligible male relative. [ib281](https://binbaz.org.sa/audios/281/02-من-قوله-فان-انستم-منهم-رشدا-فادفعوا-اليهم-اموالهم) contains Ibn Kathir’s text followed by Ibn Baz’s own explicit explanation involving marriage payment and family expenditure; the article attributes the latter to Ibn Baz. [q4124](https://quran.com/4/124) supports the distinct faith/action/reward subject, not estate arithmetic. Example arithmetic is Bayan’s illustration under expressly fixed assumptions, not a personal fatwa.

**Language comparison:** Compared heirs, net-estate assumptions, absence of barriers, both sets of numbers, qualified rationale and refusal to infer income-adjusted shares across all languages.

**Unresolved:** No actual estate assessment or complete inheritance manual is provided. Neglected maintenance duties and unusual family arrangements need personal qualified advice. Independent scholar and native-language review remain pending.

## 11. What about those who never heard?

[Full article in all three languages](Bayan-Hard-Questions-master.md#never-heard)

**Original answer:** The Quran links punishment to the coming of a messenger and describes messengers as establishing proof for people. Ibn Baz explains that those unreached by the message are tested on the Day of Judgment, with salvation for obedience and punishment for refusal.

**Finding:** The extracted original passage gives the test view but omits qualifications later in the same fatwa and does not address what reaching a message means.

**Change:** Restored the distinction between general accountability and individual judgment, labelled the preferred view, preserved the same-source difference about children and recorded the unresolved threshold for distorted encounters.

**Source review:** [q1715](https://quran.com/17/15) supports no punishment before a messenger; [q4165](https://quran.com/4/165) supports messengers/proof; [q144](https://quran.com/14/4) supports clarification in the people’s language, not a complete legal threshold. [ib7987](https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/7987/اهل-الفترة-وحكمهم) explicitly prefers testing, with salvation for obedience and punishment for refusal. [ib1004](https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/1004/حكم-اهل-الفترة-وما-جاء-في-والدي-الرسول-ﷺ) explains proof and testing, then expressly reports and positively evaluates an alternative salvation view for non-Muslim children. That difference is not falsely generalised to adults. [q440](https://quran.com/4/40) supports no injustice. No individual destiny, automatic salvation or nationality-based classification is inferred.

**Language comparison:** Checked that the preferred view, outcomes, restricted child discussion, alternative view and its favourable evaluation, and unknown individual threshold all survive in Arabic and German.

**Unresolved:** The exact threshold for partial or distorted information remains a specific research gap. A full attributed survey of alternative views about unreached adults is not supplied. Children are not silently equated with adults. Independent scholar and native-language acceptance remain pending.

## 12. Was Islam spread by the sword?

[Full article in all three languages](Bayan-Hard-Questions-master.md#sword)

**Original answer:** Ibn Baz reports one classical view that limits the non-compulsion verse to the People of the Book and Magians in rulings of fighting and jizya. He also reports another view that treats its earlier ruling as abrogated after fighting was legislated.

**Finding:** The original only reported two interpretations of one verse; it did not answer the history question.

**Change:** Separated conquest, conversion and law; added documentary/historical research and its limits; retained the difficult legal views and the commander report’s restrictions.

**Source review:** [princeton-docs](https://manuscripts.blogs.princeton.edu/2018/10/26/documenting-islamic-society/) supplies the Egyptian conquest date and documentary collection context. [egypt2020](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/gdc/gdcebookspublic/20/21/75/86/87/2021758687/2021758687.pdf), Introduction p. 19, distinguishes later conversion and debated chronology. [q2256](https://quran.com/2/256) and [q929](https://quran.com/9/29) establish the actual scriptural statements. [ib31487](https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/31487/هل-نسخت-اية-السيف-اية-لا-اكراه-في-الدين) reports two views; neither is silently selected as Bayan’s own strongest view. [m1731](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1731a) supplies the appointed-commander context, Islam/jizyah/fighting sequence and restrictions, not a universal historical record. Editorial distinctions between a law, an event and a global inference are Bayan’s reasoning.

**Language comparison:** Compared all three for conquest/conversion distinction, the limited historical cases, both legal views including their difficult clauses, submission wording, commander context and restrictions. No translation turns warfare into exclusively defensive war or denies coercion altogether.

**Unresolved:** This is not a comprehensive history of all regions, a global estimate of conversion motives, or a complete jurisprudential reconciliation. ## Expansion briefs and current status

The original nine briefs are retained as the research record, not evidence of measured demand. Status distinguishes completed local drafts from unfinished candidates. Full answers and current unresolved points appear in the articles above.

### 1. Why Islam rather than another religion?

**Reader concern:** Why should a thoughtful person accept Islam’s particular truth claims when other religions also offer purpose, morality and spiritual experience?

**Overlap and distinct scope:** Articles 2 and 5 clarify Creator reasoning and evidence. They do not establish Islam rather than competing revelations. New to Islam introduces beliefs but does not substitute for this comparative question.

**Original research brief:** Use the actual foundational texts of each tradition being compared, with named translations and context; specify which claim is being tested. Research the Quran’s claims and arguments, including [4:82](https://quran.com/4/82), their approved tafsir and objections. Separate the existence of a Creator, prophethood and textual transmission. Prepare a fair comparison rubric before selecting examples.

**Research difficulties:** A religious text’s own assertion is not independent authentication for a reader who has not accepted it. Avoid caricatures, claims about all believers, selective comparisons and unsupported historical claims. A full survey of every religion would exceed one article; define the comparisons openly.

**Status:** Written in English, Arabic and German; editorial comparison complete; integrated locally as a draft. Independent acceptance remains open.

### 2. What evidence supports Muhammad’s prophethood?

**Reader concern:** What makes belief in Muhammad as a messenger reasonable, beyond admiration or growing up Muslim?

**Overlap and distinct scope:** New to Islam lesson 4 explains his role; articles 5, 7 and 8 explain evidence, Quran transmission and hadith. None is a full case for prophethood.

**Original research brief:** Build a claim table distinguishing Quranic arguments, authenticated reports, contemporary/early historical evidence and later biography. Begin with [4:82](https://quran.com/4/82) as an argument requiring explanation and [18:110](https://quran.com/18/110) as the stated role, not independent proof. Research specific miracle and character reports with grades, exact wording and named scholarly explanations; evaluate each proposed inference.

**Research difficulties:** Avoid circular proof, ungraded popular stories and invented predictions. Historical reports have different evidential strengths. Distinguish the case for sincerity from the case for revelation; address alternative explanations fairly without pretending one anecdote settles everything.

**Status:** Written in English, Arabic and German; editorial comparison complete; integrated locally as a draft. Independent acceptance remains open.

### 3. If Allah knows and decrees everything, do our choices matter?

**Reader concern:** Am I really responsible if Allah already knows what I will do and has decreed events? Why try?

**Overlap and distinct scope:** Article 1 addresses responsibility and purpose, article 3 suffering. New to Islam lesson 7 names decree without settling freedom and accountability.

**Original research brief:** Read [81:28–29](https://quran.com/81/28-29) together, preserving both human willing and dependence on Allah. Research the full decree reports in Muslim 8a and the reports about acting rather than relying on destiny; verify exact passages and approved explanations from Ibn Baz or Ibn Uthaymeen. Separate knowledge, willing, creation and coercion before explaining their relationships.

**Research difficulties:** An analogy about foreknowledge alone does not address the whole doctrine of decree. Avoid both denying human responsibility and describing human choice as independent of Allah. Preserve the named explanation instead of inventing a philosophical reconciliation.

**Status:** Written in English, Arabic and German; editorial comparison complete; integrated locally as a draft. Independent acceptance remains open.

### 4. Why does dua sometimes seem unanswered?

**Reader concern:** I asked sincerely and nothing changed. Was I ignored, did I do something wrong, or was the promise untrue?

**Overlap and distinct scope:** Article 3 treats suffering; New to Islam lesson 18 teaches dua. This article should examine expectation, apparent nonresponse and evidence rather than repeat how to make dua.

**Original research brief:** Read [2:186](https://quran.com/2/186) in context and [Muslim 2735c](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2735c), including prohibited requests and impatience. Research the exact grading and wording of reports about different forms of response, rather than quoting an unchecked popular summary. Obtain approved commentary explaining the promise and its conditions.

**Research difficulties:** Do not blame a particular sufferer or claim to know why a request was not visibly granted. Do not promise a timetable, guaranteed material result or psychological cure. Distinguish authenticated teaching from practical emotional support.

**Status:** Written in English, Arabic and German; editorial comparison complete; integrated locally as a draft. Independent acceptance remains open.

### 5. How should I understand eternal punishment and justice?

**Reader concern:** How can unending punishment be just for choices made in a finite life, especially with unequal access to understanding?

**Overlap and distinct scope:** Articles 3 and 11 address justice and reaching the message. This question concerns duration, proportionality and the actual scope of punishment texts.

**Original research brief:** Map audiences and conditions in [4:168–169](https://quran.com/4/168-169), the justice principle in [4:40](https://quran.com/4/40), and other relevant Quran passages. Research authentic intercession reports and approved tafsir before distinguishing different categories of people and outcomes. Any attributed disagreement about duration needs its own exact primary scholarly passage.

**Research difficulties:** Avoid applying one punishment passage to every sinner, erasing explicit duration, or converting an attributed rationale into an exhaustive divine explanation. Do not pronounce individual relatives’ destinations. Philosophical proportionality objections need an actual response, not only a repeated assertion of justice.

**Status:** Written in English, Arabic and German; editorial comparison complete; integrated locally as a draft. Independent acceptance remains open.

### 6. What does Quran 4:34 mean, particularly concerning harm and abuse?

**Reader concern:** Does the verse permit a husband to harm or control his wife, and how do its words and explanations relate to her safety and dignity?

**Overlap and distinct scope:** Article 9 addresses spiritual standing and explicitly does not settle this passage. New to Islam modesty and family guidance do not cover marital harm.

**Original research brief:** Read the Arabic and named translations of [4:34–35](https://quran.com/an-nisa/34-35), not just one English rendering. Compare recognised tafsir and approved scholars on qiwamah, nushuz, the disputed phrase, restrictions and arbitration. Include [Muslim 2328a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2328a) in its exact scope. Research reliable harm-related reports, named scholarly answers about injury and remedies, and suitable present-day support information if the article offers it.

**Research difficulties:** Do not silently replace a classical interpretation with a preferred translation, or treat a restriction as permission for abuse. Avoid turning an educational explanation into a personal instruction to remain in danger. Distinguish historical law, attributed interpretation and any jurisdiction-specific legal guidance, which needs current official evidence.

**Status:** Written in English, Arabic and German; editorial comparison complete; integrated locally as a draft. Independent acceptance remains open.

### 7. How should questions about Aisha’s age and marriage be examined?

**Reader concern:** What do the reports actually say, how reliable are alternative chronologies, and how should the moral concern be addressed honestly?

**Overlap and distinct scope:** Article 8 explains transmission methods, while New to Islam lesson 4 introduces the Prophet. Neither supplies this historical and ethical examination.

**Original research brief:** Begin with [Bukhari 5134](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5134) and [Muslim 1422a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1422a), distinguishing marriage contract and consummation. Trace chains, wording and named scholarly assessments. Test alternative age calculations against their own source dates and reliability. Historical customs or medical-development claims require suitable independent evidence.

**Research difficulties:** Do not conceal the reported ages, assume a speculative calculation disproves an authentic report, or invent statements about Aisha’s physical or psychological development. “It was normal then” requires evidence and does not alone answer the moral objection. Keep historical explanation distinct from present-day marriage guidance.

**Status:** Written in English, Arabic and German; editorial comparison complete; integrated locally as a draft. Independent acceptance remains open.

### 8. How do religious freedom and classical apostasy rulings relate?

**Reader concern:** Can a person honestly examine belief if leaving carries a penalty in classical law? How does that relate to no compulsion?

**Overlap and distinct scope:** Article 12 presents difficult material about conquest and coercion without settling apostasy. Article 4 distinguishes questions and unwanted thoughts from a religious-status judgment.

**Original research brief:** Read [2:256](https://quran.com/2/256), [Bukhari 6922](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6922) and [Muslim 1676a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1676a) with context. Research approved scholars’ definitions, evidentiary conditions, authority, repentance and distinctions between cases. Any claim that the rule concerns only political treason must be tested against the actual sources. Modern rights or state-law comparisons require current primary legal texts.

**Research difficulties:** Do not soften or hide the death-penalty material, or equate sincere questioning with a legal verdict. Separate what a source says, how it was interpreted and what a particular state enacted. No personal enforcement guidance or verdict about an individual belongs in the article. The moral and legal tension must be addressed directly.

**Status:** Written in English, Arabic and German; editorial comparison complete; integrated locally as a draft. Independent acceptance remains open.

### 9. How should slavery in Islamic texts and history be understood?

**Reader concern:** Why do the texts recognise ownership of people, including sexual relations with enslaved women, and how does that relate to dignity and freedom?

**Overlap and distinct scope:** Article 12 distinguishes texts, law and historical conduct. Article 9’s spiritual standing does not answer ownership, consent or enslavement.

**Original research brief:** Map texts encouraging emancipation, including [90:11–16](https://quran.com/al-balad/11-16), alongside texts recognising ownership, including [23:5–6](https://quran.com/23/5-6). Research authenticated reports and approved explanations of acquisition, emancipation, treatment, sexual relations and relevant restrictions. Use primary documentary research for actual historical practices and official evidence for modern abolition and trafficking law if discussed.

**Research difficulties:** Do not equate enslavement with ordinary employment, suggest encouragement to free slaves was itself an immediate universal ban, or avoid the questions of coercion and consent. Historical improvements do not by themselves resolve the ethical objection. Define eras and legal categories; contemporary claims need current evidence. No practical enslavement guidance belongs here.

**Status:** Written in English, Arabic and German; editorial comparison complete; integrated locally as a draft. Independent acceptance remains open.
