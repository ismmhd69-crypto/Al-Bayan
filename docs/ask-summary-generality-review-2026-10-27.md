## Instruction changes

- Shared meaning rules clarify that an expressly general principle may support faithful paraphrases and narrower instances within its actual scope. Missing vocabulary alone does not prove missing meaning. Examples alone never establish a new general rule. New procedures, outcomes, causes, legal effects and personal rulings still require explicit support.
- The summary writer starts with a substantive sourced statement, rather than an isolated yes/no label. It compares correction feedback against the original instead of treating feedback as a new religious fact or inventing a source-silence disclaimer solely from a missing word.
- Primary clause review keeps answer polarity, attribution and negation attached to the assertion they qualify, and reads the whole sentence and question before assessing each clause. No received negative verdict is rewritten, no claim text is stripped and no review is bypassed.

This is a prompt/judgment clarification, not a deterministic replay repair. All citation, copy, context, condition, scope, personal-application and coverage validators, correction allowances and spending limits remain. Changed meaning instructions change the request fingerprint, so prior meaning approvals do not stand in for fresh checks under changed instructions.
