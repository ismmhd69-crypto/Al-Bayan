### Primary Verified Scholar Fatwa

From binbaz.org.sa, the core source is fatwa 31487 by Shaikh Abd al-Aziz ibn Baz (`S9d295174-07ff-4455-8952-776ed3a1c094`), titled: "هل نسخت آية السيف آية {لَا إِكْرَاهَ فِي الدِّينِ}؟":

"قيل: منسوخة. وقيل: إنها في أهل الكتاب خاصةً. فيها قولان: أحدهما أنها في أهل الكتاب والمجوس، لا يُكرهون، بل يُخيرون بين الإسلام والجزية، فإن لم يُؤدوا الجزيةَ ولا الإسلام قُوتلوا، وأما بقية الناس فلا تخيير: إما الإسلام، وإما السيف. وقال آخرون: بل هي منسوخة، كان هذا أول الأمر، ثم شرع الله القتال والجهاد؛ لقوله سبحانه: وَقَاتِلُوهُمْ حَتَّى لَا تَكُونَ فِتْنَةٌ وَيَكُونَ الدِّينُ كُلُّهُ لِلَّهِ [الأنفال:39]، فنُسخت بهذه الآية وما جاء في معناها."

The fatwa presents two classical scholarly positions on the relationship between Surah 2:256 ("there is no compulsion in religion") and the verses of fighting:

1. **The First View (Specificity to People of the Book and Magians):**
   The verse is specific to the People of the Book and the Magians: they are not coerced, but rather given the choice between Islam and paying the jizya (Surah 9:29). If they pay neither jizya nor accept Islam, they are fought.
   As for the rest of people (all other polytheists), there is no choice: either Islam, or the sword ("وأما بقية الناس فلا تخيير: إما الإسلام، وإما السيف").

2. **The Second View (Abrogation by the Command of Fighting and Jihad):**
   The verse is abrogated. Not compelling people applied only at the beginning of the prophetic era, before Allah legislated fighting and jihad through Surah 8:39 ("And fight them until there is no fitnah and religion is wholly for Allah") and related verses such as the sword verse (Surah 9:5).

### Investigation of binbaz.org.sa and binothaimeen.net

As instructed, I searched binbaz.org.sa and binothaimeen.net for fatwas on the purpose of jihad, the treatment of non-combatants, and whether belief is forced into the heart:

1. **Purpose of Jihad and Defense:**
   In Discussion 27 on binbaz.org.sa ("ليس الجهاد للدفاع فقط") and Fatwa 2160, Shaikh Ibn Baz explicitly argues against modern writers who claim that jihad was legislated solely for defense. He affirms that jihad was legislated to convey Islam and remove polytheism, stating that polytheists other than the People of the Book and Magians are fought until they enter Islam.
2. **Treatment of Non-Combatants:**
   In his commentary on hadith texts in Kitab al-Jihad, Shaikh Ibn Baz notes that the Prophet forbade killing women and children because they are not combatants. However, neither he nor Shaikh Ibn Uthaymeen treats this restriction as softening the classical ruling on fighting combatant polytheists until they submit or enter Islam.
3. **Belief in the Heart:**
   In fatwa 2160, Shaikh Ibn Baz addresses Surah 2:256 and explicitly rejects the argument that non-Muslims cannot be compelled, arguing instead that compelling polytheists to enter Islam rescues them from eternal destruction, and stating that this applies to all except those eligible for jizya.

Because the approved scholars do not use these concepts to contradict or soften the ruling in fatwa 31487, no outside reconciliations or apologetic claims (such as asserting that "fighting was legislated only to end persecution" or "removing oppression rather than coercing belief") were added. Similarly, Surah 60:8 was removed from the sources because it was not part of the scholar's explanation in fatwa 31487.
